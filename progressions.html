Fingerings & strum

Diagrams are the neck held upright in front of you: thickest string on the left, nut at the top. Numbers are fingers (1 index, 2 middle, 3 ring, 4 pinky). A hollow circle means play the string open, an × means skip it, a bar is your index finger across several strings.

96

The strum: down, down-up, up-down-up

The first pattern everybody learns, and it fits nearly every song here. Your strumming hand swings down on the numbers and up on the "ands" the whole time; the pattern only decides which swings touch the strings.

1&2&3&4&

↓ down through all the strings  ·  ↑ up through the top three or four  ·  · keep the hand moving, miss the strings.

4Put a capo on. Play the same shapes.

A capo is a clamp across all six strings at one fret. Your fingers keep making the same shapes; the whole guitar just moves up. One fret up is one half step up. That is the entire rule.

Tap a fret.

Why anyone bothers. Guitars are easy in C, G, D, A and E because those keys use open strings. Singers do not care about that, and plenty of songs live in B♭, E♭ or F. Without a capo those keys mean barre chords all the way through. With one, they mean the chords you learned first, moved up.

The other direction. A song too high to sing? Take the capo off or move it down. Too low? Move it up. Same shapes, same fingers, new key.

Putting it on. Clamp just behind the fret wire, not on top of it, and only as tight as it needs to be. Squeeze too hard and every string goes sharp.
