Structure

A shape to
hang it on.

Four chords in a loop is a foundation. The chorus below is built out of your verse, not pulled out of a hat.

A verse is where the story goes and it repeats with different words. The chorus is the part everyone sings, same words every time, usually higher. A pre-chorus is the short ramp between them. A bridge shows up once, late, to break the pattern before the last chorus. Verse, verse, chorus, verse, chorus, bridge, chorus is a whole song.

Parts for the room

Four people,
four things to play.

When six guitars all strum full chords it turns to mud. Hand these out and the same progression starts sounding arranged.

Two words that come up below. The root is the note a chord is named after: the G in a G chord. It is the lowest note in every open shape on this site. Pentatonic means five notes, chosen so that none of them clash with any chord in the key. That is why a beginner can wander around the box below and nothing sounds wrong.
